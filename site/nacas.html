Neuroanatomy and Computation Across Species (NACAS)

Comparative discussions on cognition, with an emphasis on neuroanatomy and computation.

Organisers

Seán Froudist-Walsh and Nima Khalighinejad

About the series

Once per term, NACAS welcomes one or more researchers working on related topics across species to enable comparative discussions on cognition, with an emphasis on neuroanatomy and computation.

The series brings together researchers across Oxford Neuroscience interested in approaches that connect scales, species, cognition and computation. Alongside the main seminars, early career researchers can take part in journal clubs and lunch-with-speaker events.

Neuroanatomy and Computation Across Species

Cowey Rooms, FMRIB Annexe, Oxford

James Pang (Monash University) discussed how cortical shape and connection patterns help explain variation in brain dynamics across mammals.

10 March 2026 · 12:00–13:30

Symbols in the brain and the limits of non-human primate cognition

Life and Mind Building, Seminar room 6, Oxford

Lucas Tian (Rockefeller University, New York) presented work in which monkeys learned to draw geometric figures by recombining action symbols, examining how frontal cortex represents these symbols and their grammar.

Mathias Sablé-Meyer (UCL) discussed differences in the representation of geometric shapes across human adults, infants and baboons, and their implications for potentially human-specific abilities.

The talks were followed by comparative discussion on symbolic representations and the limits of non-human primate cognition.
